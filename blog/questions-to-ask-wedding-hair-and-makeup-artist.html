24 Questions to Ask Your Wedding Hair and Makeup Artist

By Andreina · Updated September 22, 2026 · 8 min read

You have a shortlist. Three artists, maybe five, all with beautiful feeds and kind replies to your inquiry — and no obvious way to tell which one will actually show up on time, work calmly through a room of eight people, and hand you back a face that still looks like you at midnight. Instagram cannot tell you that. The right questions to ask a wedding hair and makeup artist can.

The short answer

Before you book, ask about experience with your skin tone and hair texture, exactly what the price includes, whether a trial is part of the package, who will personally be doing your hair and makeup, how long each person takes, and what happens if your artist cannot make it. Get every answer in writing.

Below are the twenty-four that actually change the outcome of your morning, grouped the way I would ask them, with my own answers underneath. Steal the list. Send it to every artist on your shortlist. The way someone answers question six will tell you more than any portfolio.

Start with experience and style

Talent and bridal experience are not the same skill. A brilliant editorial artist who has never worked a wedding morning may not know how to keep six people on schedule while the photographer needs the bride in twenty minutes.

  1. How long have you been doing bridal hair and makeup specifically? Weddings are their own discipline — long wear, unpredictable lighting, and a hard deadline that does not move.
  2. Can I see a full gallery, not just your favorites? Anyone can produce five stunning photos. Ask for a range: different ages, different lighting, indoors and outdoors.
  3. How would you describe your style? Natural, soft glam, and full glam are genuinely different results. If an artist's portfolio is all heavy contour and you want barely-there skin, that is a mismatch, not a fixable preference.
  4. Have you worked with my skin tone and my hair texture? This is not a delicate question and you should not soften it. The honest answer protects you both, and a good artist will say so plainly if something is outside their wheelhouse.

What the price actually includes

This is where most surprises live, and almost all of them are avoidable.

  1. What is in the package, item by item? Hair and makeup are usually priced separately. Confirm whether lashes are included and whether the quoted number is per person or per service.
  2. Is the trial included in the price, or billed separately? Mine is billed separately — $175 for a hair trial, $175 for a makeup trial — because a trial is real chair time, not a free sample.
  3. What do bridesmaids, mothers, and flower girls cost? My bridal party rate is $125 per person for hair and $125 for makeup. Flower girls seven and under are $65 for hair and $25 for makeup. Clip-in extensions that you already own are $50 to attach.
  4. Is there a travel fee, and how is it calculated? Ask for the actual formula, not a vague "depends on distance." Mine runs on mileage bands from my Rockwall studio: $45 for 1–30 miles, $65 for 31–50, $85 for 51–70, and $105 for 71–90. Parking and tolls are added only when I actually incur them.

Here is the one opinion I will plant my flag on: if an artist will not put the complete price in writing before you pay a deposit, that is your answer. Not a red flag to investigate — an answer. Every rate I charge is published on my bridal hair and makeup pricing page where anyone can read it before they ever message me, including the travel fee bands. I did that on purpose. A bride comparing three artists should be able to compare three real numbers, and the artist who makes that hard is usually the one whose invoice grows between booking and the wedding.

The trial

The trial is not a luxury add-on. It is the entire reason the wedding morning stays calm.

  1. When should we schedule the trial? One to three months out is the sweet spot — close enough that your hair length and skin are roughly what they will be, far enough that there is room to adjust.
  2. What is included in a bridal hair and makeup trial? In mine, we build the full look together, test it against your dress and your jewelry, and change whatever does not feel right while you are still in the chair.
  3. What should I bring? Inspiration photos, your veil or hairpiece, a top in a similar neckline and color to your dress, and any extensions you plan to wear.
  4. How do you remember the look for the wedding day? Ask this one. I photograph the finished trial from several angles and write down the products, so the day-of look is a recreation rather than a fresh attempt.

Who shows up, and how the morning runs

  1. Will you personally be doing my hair and makeup? Some artists take the inquiry and send an associate. Neither model is wrong, but you should know which one you are buying.
  2. Can you handle my entire party? One artist has a real ceiling. When a party is bigger than I can finish comfortably on schedule, I bring in Beauty on Demand, a fellow on-location artist based in Rockwall, so nobody is rushed and nobody is waiting in a robe at noon.
  3. How long do you need per person? You cannot build a wedding morning timeline without this number. Ask for it per service, then let your artist help you sequence the order.
  4. Will you help place my veil and accessories? A veil set by the person who built the style holds better than one pinned in afterward by a helpful aunt.

How the look holds up — products, longevity, and Texas heat

  1. What products do you use, and can you work around sensitivities? Tell your artist about eczema, rosacea, sensitive eyes, or a reaction you have had before. There is almost always a workaround, but only if we know before the wedding morning.
  2. Do you offer airbrush, and would you recommend it for me? The right answer is a recommendation based on your skin, not a blanket yes.
  3. How do you make a look survive an outdoor Texas ceremony? Ask this specifically if you are marrying here between May and September. The answer should involve skin prep and product choice, not just a spritz of setting spray at the end. Humidity does not care how good your foundation is if the skin underneath was never prepped for it.
  4. Do I get a touch-up kit, or can you stay for touch-ups? Both are reasonable answers. What matters is knowing which one you are getting before the day.

The questions most brides forget to ask

These four are the difference between a vendor and a professional, and they are the clearest signs of a good wedding vendor to book.

  1. What happens if you get sick or your car breaks down? Every full-time artist has a plan. Ask what it is. "That has never happened to me" is not a plan.
  2. How do you clean your brushes between clients? A professional will answer this instantly and in detail, because they already have a system.
  3. What do you need from me in the getting-ready room? Nobody asks this and everybody should. I need a chair without arms at a normal table height, an outlet within reach, and the best natural light in the building — which is usually a window, not the bathroom with the flattering vanity bulbs. A room set up before I arrive saves fifteen minutes that you will want back later.
  4. Do we sign a contract, and what is the cancellation policy? Mine is a $25 non-refundable deposit per person to hold the date, 48 hours notice to cancel or reschedule, and a $15 late fee — all published in writing before anyone pays anything.

Common Questions

Wedding Hair and Makeup FAQ

How far in advance should you book wedding hair and makeup?

Six to twelve months before the wedding, and closer to twelve if your date is a Saturday between May and October. Popular DFW dates go early. Book the artist first and schedule the trial one to three months out, once the date is secured.

Do you tip your wedding makeup artist?

A tip is never expected and always appreciated. The commonly cited range is 15 to 20 percent of the service total, handed over on the wedding morning or added when you settle the final invoice.

Do makeup artists travel to the venue?

Many do, and on-location artists like me do it by default — I come to your venue, home, or getting-ready suite anywhere across the Dallas–Fort Worth area. Always confirm the travel fee and how it is calculated before you book.

What is included in a bridal hair and makeup trial?

A full build of your wedding-day look, tested against your dress, veil, and jewelry, with adjustments made while you are still in the chair. A good trial ends with the look photographed and the products written down, so the wedding morning is a recreation and not an experiment.

How long does wedding hair and makeup take per person?

It varies by artist and by service, which is why you should ask for the number rather than assume one. Once you have it, you can build a realistic wedding morning timeline backward from your photographer's first-look time.

What if my hair and makeup artist cancels?

This is why question 21 matters. Ask before booking who covers your date if your artist cannot, and make sure your contract says what happens to your deposit. If my date is already taken, I point brides toward artists I trust rather than leaving them to start over.

One last thing

If you only ask three of these, ask what the full price includes, who will personally be doing your hair, and what the backup plan is. Those three answers, given clearly and without hedging, tell you almost everything about how the morning will go.
